Design system · v2

The sun, turned all the way up.

v1 wrote the brand down and asked it to stay calm. v2 gives it a voice: a display face with character, light and orbit as a visual language, and the parts the client portal was missing.

What v2 changes

Four v1 rules made everything flat. All four are retired. The palette, the mark and the voice are untouched.

Typography

One family became three faces.

v1 ran display and text on the same system stack, so nothing on the page had a voice. Sora now carries every headline. Text and mono are unchanged.

Visual language

"One gradient exists in this brand."

Now five, plus glows, glass and texture — all tokens. Light and orbit is the signature, and it is made of light rather than more colour.

Accent budget

Twice per screen became two rules.

Action ember — a solid fill you press — stays one per screen. Light ember — glow, gradient, orbit, lit edge — is unlimited.

Motion

Ambient drift became five signatures.

Orbit, light sweep, scroll reveal, count-up, hover glow. Each one says something. All of them stop dead under reduced motion.

Carried in as a fix

v1's gradient headline word used --sun1 as text colour in both themes. On the light ground that measures 1.60:1 — a failure, on the largest words on the page. v2 gives the effect its own token, --grad-headline, whose worst stop is 3.4:1 light and 6.9:1 dark at display size.

The mark

A sun, one orbit ring, one agent dot. v2 changes nothing here — the mark was never the problem.

The rule

One ring, one dot.

Two dots is a different mark. The dot always breaks the ring on the upper right — it is one agent in orbit, and it sits outside the sun so it stays visible at small sizes.

Colour

Unchanged from v1. Every number below is measured against the real background, in the theme you are looking at.

Type

Three roles, three faces. The single biggest reason v1 read as flat was that nothing on the page had a voice.

Display · Sora

Why this face

A geometric grotesque whose bowls are near-perfect circles — the same geometry as the mark — with tight apertures and flat terminals that read as technical rather than friendly. It holds its presence at 96px, which is what the big statement needs.

And it is not Inter, Roboto or Space Grotesk, so it does not arrive pre-loaded with somebody else's startup.

400–800 variable swap no blank page OFL 1.1 licence

Follow-up · brand/build.py needs the same face

build.py writes SVG banners, and an SVG cannot load a webfont — it renders with whatever the viewer has. Until this is dealt with, the banners are set in the system grotesque and the site is set in Sora, and they read as two brands.

Recommended: outline the lockup to <path> data once, and leave generated body labels on the system stack. Embedding a base64 subset also works but inflates every banner. Until one of them lands, build.py's own stack stays as it is — a half-migration that merely references Sora would render as a random fallback on other people's machines, which is worse.

Light and orbit

The signature language. Four materials, stacked in a fixed order: light, ground, orbit, glass.

The core rule, enforced structurally

Ambient light is position: absolute inside a section that is relative, overflow: hidden and isolation: isolate. Never fixed. That is what keeps the core out from under panel text — and it is a bug fix: v1 pinned a 78vmax sun to the viewport, so it tracked the reader down the entire page and sat under whatever they were reading.

Measured: --faint through dark glass over a glow halo is 4.9:1 and passes. Over the core it is 4.1:1 and fails. So the core does not go there.

The accent budget

v1 made the brand's only warm colour a scarce resource, so nothing glowed. v2 splits the accent by what it does.

The test

Could a reader mistake this for something to press?

If yes, it is action ember and it costs the budget. If no, it is light, and it is free. A glowing 1px orbit line is not a button. A 48px pill with a label is.

Motion

Five signatures. Each one says something. All of them stop dead under prefers-reduced-motion, and every value stays on the screen.

Reduced motion, explicitly

Orbits stop — the rings stay, they do not turn. The sweep does not run. Reveals are skipped, never left at opacity: 0 waiting for an observer. Count-ups jump to their value. Parallax is off. Nothing in this system is load-bearing on animation.

Data visualisation

Four components for the client portal. Inline SVG and CSS off the chart tokens — no chart library, nothing to keep up to date.

Every figure on this page is example data for a made-up bakery, and it is labelled as such. This system does not print a number it cannot stand behind.

Section archetypes

v1 had one shape — a card grid — which is the other half of why the pages felt mediocre. Six shapes, each with a job. No two adjacent sections use the same one.

1 · Full-bleed feature — one per page. The hero at the top of this page is the live example.

Edge to edge

For the one thing the page is actually about.

Its own light source, its own orbit rings, its own ground. Content inset to --container-wide, corners at --radius-2xl. Nothing else on the page competes with it.

2 · Split with visual — prose one side, the thing being described on the other.

Two columns from 900px

The visual is never decoration.

Prose capped at --measure on one side; a real chart, panel or working control on the other. Below 900px the prose goes first, always — never a picture the reader has to scroll past to reach the point.

If there is nothing real to show, it is a card, not a split.
